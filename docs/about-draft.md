## Where the words and pictures come from

Every page is adapted from Wikipedia and shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each page links the exact revision it
was written from. Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under their own free licences; the credit is under
each photo. Images may be cropped, resized, rotated or animated for display.

Maps are drawn from Natural Earth (public domain). Only coastlines are drawn, never borders.

## What talks to whom

Reading the morning and evening pages sends nothing anywhere: the text, the photos and the maps are all
served by Margin itself. There is no tracking, no analytics, no cookies and no account.

**The Rabbit Trail is the one exception.** When you follow a trail, your browser asks Wikipedia directly
(en.wikipedia.org, de.wikipedia.org or fr.wikipedia.org) which articles the current one links to and
what they say. That means Wikipedia sees those requests and your IP address, under the
[Wikimedia Foundation's privacy policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Privacy_policy).
The requests carry no cookies and load no pictures. Answers are kept in your browser for up to a week,
so the same trail isn't asked for twice. If Wikipedia can't be reached, the trail uses pages that were
saved into Margin in advance, and Wikipedia isn't asked again for the rest of that trail.

The trail only offers stops that pass the same checks as the daily pages: no lists or disambiguation
pages, no living people, and none of the topics Margin keeps out (graphic violence, explicit content,
drugs, live political controversy, and, on trails, articles about wars, weapons and crimes themselves).

## What stays on your device

Margin notes, kept trails and your settings live in your browser's storage and nowhere else. Clearing
your browser's data for this site removes them.
